1. The Lattice Metric System

The foundation of crystallography lies in transforming unitless fractional coordinates $(\xi, \eta, \zeta)$ into physical Cartesian space $(x, y, z)$. This is performed via the Lattice Matrix $\mathbf{M}$.

\[ \mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \cos \gamma & c \cos \beta \\ 0 & b \sin \gamma & c \frac{\cos \alpha - \cos \beta \cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma} \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{V}{ab \sin \gamma} \end{bmatrix} \]

Where $V$ is the unit cell volume: \[ V = abc \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \alpha - \cos^2 \beta - \cos^2 \gamma + 2 \cos \alpha \cos \beta \cos \gamma} \]

2. Algebraic Symmetry Parsing

Our engine avoids eval() for security and precision. It decomposes symmetry strings like "x+1/2, -y, z+1/4" into a matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and a translation vector $\mathbf{t}$.

\[ \mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{S} \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{t} \] Example: "x+1/2, -y, z" $\rightarrow$ \[ \mathbf{S} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{t} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

3. The Multi-Pass Algorithm

1

Primitive Deduplication

We first filter the asymmetric unit in fractional space. Two sites are merged if their fractional distance is less than $10^{-4}$.

\[ \Delta \xi_{fract} < 10^{-4} \]
2

Symmetry Expansion & PBC Wrapping

For every operator $\mathbf{S}_i$, we generate new coordinates and fold them back into the cell boundaries $[0, 1)$ using the modulo protocol.

\[ \xi_{wrapped} = (\xi \pmod 1 + 1) \pmod 1 \]
3

The Cartesian Collision Guard

This is the critical "Burai-parity" step. Because periodic boundaries can cause floating-point drift (e.g., $0.9999$ vs $0.0001$), we convert to Cartesian space before the final check.

Two atoms are merged if the Euclidean distance $R$ satisfies:

\[ R = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2 + (z_2-z_1)^2} < 0.001 \, \text{\AA} \]

4. Why Cartesian Deduplication?

In non-orthogonal cells (Hexagonal, Monoclinic), simple fractional equality tests fail to account for the physical proximity of atoms at boundaries. By enforcing a 0.001 Å threshold in physical space, our parser achieves exact parity with professional software like Burai, eliminating "ghost atoms" and ensuring stable Quantum ESPRESSO simulations.
