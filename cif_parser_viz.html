1
Asymmetric Unit Injection
Extracting the linearly independent atomic sites from the CIF file. This is the minimal geometric basis of the crystal.
\( \mathbf{R}_{prim} = \xi\mathbf{a} + \eta\mathbf{b} + \zeta\mathbf{c} \)
where \( \xi, \eta, \zeta \in [0, 1) \)
2
Space Group Expansion
Applying algebraic symmetry operators to generate the full unit cell. Notice how some generated atoms fall outside the primary bounding box.
\( \begin{pmatrix} \xi' \\ \eta' \\ \zeta' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} S_{11} & S_{12} & S_{13} \\ S_{21} & S_{22} & S_{23} \\ S_{31} & S_{32} & S_{33} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \eta \\ \zeta \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} \tau_x \\ \tau_y \\ \tau_z \end{pmatrix} \)
3
Periodic Boundary Enforcement
Applying modulo arithmetic to fold coordinates back into the primary Wigner-Seitz cell (the central box). Watch the exterior atoms wrap around.
\( \mathbf{r}_{wrap} = \mathbf{r}' - \lfloor \mathbf{r}' \rfloor \)
using the floor function for \( \pmod 1 \)
4
Cartesian Collision Guard
The crucial Burai deduplication step. Because of wrapping, multiple symmetry operations may map to the exact same physical site (e.g., at faces/corners). We scan Euclidean space and merge atoms that overlap.
\( \mathbf{r}_{cart} = \mathbf{M} \cdot \mathbf{r}_{wrap} \)
\( d = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2 + \Delta z^2} < 0.001 \text{ \AA} \)
